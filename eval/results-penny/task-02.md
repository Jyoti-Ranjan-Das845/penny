# Special Committee on the COVID-19 Pandemic — Sixth Meeting

**Summary for policy readers.** Videoconference sitting of the House of Commons Special Committee on the COVID-19 Pandemic, chaired by Hon. Anthony Rota. The transcript is undated; internal references place it in mid-May 2020 — the large employer emergency financing facility (LEEFF) was announced "yesterday," the one-time OAS payment was introduced that day, and a Wet'suwet'en MOU announcement was scheduled for May 14. No ministerial announcements were made. The session ran petitions (15 minutes) followed by questions to ministers.

## The main exchange: CERB fraud screening

Dan Albas (CPC) pressed the Prime Minister on a same-day media report that federal staff had been instructed to pay CERB claims despite flags of likely fraud, and asked him to confirm 200,000 flagged applications and to say whether his office signed off on a deputy minister's memo. Across seven exchanges Trudeau neither confirmed nor denied the memo, the number, or sign-off, restating that the government chose speed over front-end verification and would "clean it up after the fact," including retroactive action on fraudulent claims. Pressed on whether every case would be pursued, he committed only in general terms: "We knew there would be a need to clean up after the fact, to go after fraudulent cases. We will do that."

Alain Rayes (CPC) put the same question to the Minister of National Revenue. Diane Lebouthillier answered directly: **no**, the CRA did not send such a memo. The transcript therefore contains a flat denial from the revenue minister alongside a Prime Minister who declined to engage the specifics — a gap worth tracking against the originating memo.

## Program design questions the government did not resolve

- **CERB repayment on return to work.** Rayes asked four times whether a worker returning to work repays $500 per week worked or the full $2,000 monthly payment, noting officials would not answer technical calls. Carla Qualtrough said Service Canada would work case-by-case to avoid overpayment and undue hardship, but stated no rule. Employers deciding on wage-subsidy uptake and workers deciding whether to return were left without the number seven weeks into the program.
- **Right to refuse work.** Asked whether employees may refuse to return to a compliant workplace and keep CERB, Qualtrough pointed to existing federal and provincial refusal-to-work processes and said CERB is "there for workers who aren't working due to COVID-19" — no clarification of the interaction.
- **CERB eligibility gap.** Jenny Kwan (NDP) raised a single parent of four who lost taxable spousal and child support to COVID-19 and is ineligible for CERB; the Employment Minister had been aware for three weeks without action. Trudeau pointed to the Canada child benefit top-up and 7+ million CERB recipients (Lebouthillier cited 7.8 million applicants); no fix was announced.
- **Fish harvesters.** Bernadette Jordan confirmed harvesters are excluded from the wage subsidy by the related-employee rule, which disqualifies most family-run enterprises, and that the $62.5 million package went to processors. She said direct harvester support is being developed and she hoped to say more "very soon." Chris d'Entremont (CPC) cited losses of up to 95% of Asian lobster markets. Jordan also confirmed that catch-limit conditions added to 2021 gulf lobster licences were **not** authorized by her, were removed, and "will not happen."

## PPE and procurement

Stephanie Kusie (CPC) cited Public Services and Procurement Canada figures: 1.8 billion PPE units procured, but under 6% of N95 orders, under 8% of surgical mask orders and 1.4% of face shield orders filled; she also cited expired PPE in Saskatchewan, faulty PPE causing rashes and headaches in Alberta, 34 Chinese suppliers, and an academic assessment that the national emergency strategic stockpile had failed. Anita Anand attributed the gap to global competition and production delays, cited multiple parallel supply chains and over two million face shields delivered, and said front-line health workers are the first priority with essential-services coverage under exploration. No second-wave PPE plan was tabled, against Abacus polling Kusie cited showing 69% of Canadians expect a second wave.

## Other substantive items

- **LEEFF tax conditions.** Gabriel Ste-Marie (BQ) argued the tax-haven condition catches only abusive evasion and avoidance, leaving legal tax-haven structures — used by the large banks and multinationals — eligible. Trudeau said applicants must have paid their fair share, that tax structures will be assessed case-by-case with international transparency required, and that large banks are not expected to apply. He made no commitment on Ste-Marie's separate ask to cap credit-card interchange fees at 0.3% as in Europe and Australia, deferring it to "the longer term."
- **Housing and homelessness.** Kwan sought federal matching funds for provincial hotel-acquisition efforts, a national standard for shelters and SROs, and recognition of a right to housing, noting Vancouver East received under 2% of pandemic urban Indigenous housing funding despite the third-largest urban Indigenous population. Trudeau cited hundreds of millions already sent to shelters and defended the national housing strategy's 50% chronic-homelessness reduction target; no matching commitment.
- **Seniors.** Deb Schulte introduced a one-time payment of up to $500 for OAS recipients that day, and cited an earlier GST top-up of almost $375 for single seniors and over $500 for couples. Kusie called the timing — three months in — evidence seniors are an afterthought.
- **Service Canada.** Candice Bergen (CPC) reported mail to Service Canada going unopened, including a constituent's March 12 submission with a doctor's note. Ahmed Hussen cited unprecedented volumes, centre closures and thousands of staff redeployed to phone service, said he was not aware of a widespread unopened-mail problem, and offered to look into the case.
- **Wet'suwet'en MOU.** Jamie Schmale (CPC) said elected chiefs were excluded from MOU negotiations, had asked that the May 14 announcement be halted, and were calling for Carolyn Bennett's resignation. Bennett repeatedly framed the process as Wet'suwet'en-led and declined to commit to a delay.
- **Trikafta.** Patty Hajdu confirmed Vertex has not applied to sell the cystic fibrosis drug in Canada and pointed to the special access program, under which she said most applicants have received access. She gave no availability date.

## Non-pandemic business

The committee also functioned as a general question-period substitute. Bill Blair defended the firearms order in council covering nine rifles of military provenance, citing the Canadian Association of Chiefs of Police and Ontario police leaders, against Bergen's objection that the Iver Johnson single-shot rifle is a farm gun. Petitions presented covered a national perinatal mental health strategy; rescinding the PMPRB amendments to protect access to Trikafta and other medications; the family doctor shortage (petitioners citing nearly five million Canadians without one); firearms regulation; a ban on open-net finfish aquaculture expansion in Nova Scotia (e-2512, 1,198 signatories); halting Coastal GasLink construction and implementing UNDRIP; and withholding financial assistance from airlines until COVID-19 flight refunds are paid.

## What to watch

The repayment formula for workers moving off CERB; direct support for fish harvesters; the CERB spousal/child-support gap; the originating deputy minister's memo against Lebouthillier's denial; a second-wave PPE plan; and whether the May 14 Wet'suwet'en announcement proceeded.
