# Remote control project — kickoff meeting

**Who was there:** Sarah (Project Manager, chairing), plus the Industrial Designer, User Interface, and Marketing. First meeting of the team; about 25 minutes, roughly half of it logistics and an animal-drawing icebreaker. Everything below is the substance.

## The brief
Design a new remote control that is:
- **original** — not something already sitting in the shops,
- **trendy and broadly appealing** — the Industrial Designer noted the company line is "we put the fashion into electronics," so styling counts,
- **user-friendly across ages** — from grandparents to kids.

Work runs in three stages: functional design (what needs the product must meet and how), then conceptual design, then detailed design. Members do individual work between meetings.

## Numbers given
- Selling price: €25 (the team's rough conversion was about £17; nobody was certain).
- Production cost: €12.50 — half the selling price.
- Profit target: €50 million. Marketing asked whether that's first-year; Sarah assumed so but didn't confirm.
- Market: international (Australia, America were mentioned). Sarah said multiple languages aren't a key design concern.
- For context, the Industrial Designer said remotes currently on the market run about £20, so €25 isn't out of line. Marketing raised but didn't settle whether to undercut that or risk looking like a cheap option.

## What the team said about existing remotes
- Sarah had used a universal remote to replace six handsets for three videos, a TV and an amp. Consolidation was the real benefit; it kept losing its programming and needing re-setup, possibly because it was cheap.
- Marketing only ever uses on/off, volume and channel buttons, has never programmed a remote, and said the product has to work for someone like that.
- Shared complaints: too many tiny buttons with tiny labels, and symbols users are expected to understand but don't.

## Design ideas raised
- **An LCD menu screen, phone-style** (Marketing and User Interface). Familiar navigation patterns and picture-based menus would cut button count.
- **Dual function** (Sarah's preferred framing): basic play/volume/channel buttons visible on the front, with recording, sound and other options behind a menu or a section that opens up — so it looks simple when you pick it up.
- **Accessibility for older users.** Sarah pushed back on the phone-menu idea: her grandad can answer a mobile but wouldn't attempt texting. The Industrial Designer countered that his grandad beats him at teletext, and that icon-driven menus are fairly self-evident. Marketing suggested a large-print button, as teletext already offers. Not resolved.
- **Flip-top case**, giving a bigger screen in a smaller body — flagged immediately as a cost question.
- **Materials:** plastic rather than metal — lightweight, easily moulded, cheaper. Sky and Telewest handsets came up as examples where moulding and silver plastic still look smart.
- **Cost ceiling on the screen:** at €12.50 build cost the Industrial Designer expected a black-and-white LCD at best.

## Open questions
1. First-year or cumulative profit target.
2. Whether to price at, under, or above the ~£20 market norm.
3. What a screen and a flip-top actually cost against the €12.50 budget.
4. How to keep older and non-technical users served if the design leans on menus.

## Next step
Each member went off for about 30 minutes of individual work on their own area, then the team reconvenes.
