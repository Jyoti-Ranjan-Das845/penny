# Meeting Summary: Detailed Design Review — Remote Control Prototype

This was a detailed design meeting with four participants: the Project Manager (acting as secretary), User Interface Designer David Jordan, Industrial Designer Baba, and a Marketing representative. The stated purpose was to evaluate the team's clay remote control prototype and determine whether it was ready to proceed to production. No production decision was reached: at the outset, Industrial Designer Baba signalled the overall outcome by saying, "I'm afraid this remote control will stay a prototype."

## Prototype Description

The prototype is a clay model in yellow and red — yellow as the company's brand colour, red for visual appeal. Its form is the "mushroom design": a circular ball body paired with a triangular base. Key exterior features include a fuzzy texture, a rotatable round ball for channel selection (replacing traditional buttons), mushroom-shaped buttons, and a stable triangle base. Inside the unit: a low-level chip, an LCD, and a battery housed in the base. A solar receptor is mounted on the exterior. The prototype also embeds a four-microphone array enabling voice recognition from multiple directions.

## Evaluation Scores

The team scored the prototype against three criteria using a 1–7 scale, where 1 = the criterion is fully met and 7 = the criterion is not met.

| Criterion | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fancy look and feel | 3 | Agreed by all four participants. Colour (yellow and red) was accepted, though Marketing noted too much red. Shape was judged unique but not clearly "fancy" in the fruit-and-vegetable aesthetic Marketing had set as the target. |
| Technologically innovative | 2 | Agreed by all four participants. Key features cited: the embedded four-microphone array for omnidirectional voice recognition, the novel round form factor, and tactile buttons. |
| Easy to use | Not scored — discussion not completed | Marketing introduced this criterion at the end of the meeting; the transcript ends before any score was agreed. |

The third criterion was introduced but the meeting record ends before a score was reached.
