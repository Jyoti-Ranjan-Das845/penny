# Summary: House of Commons Special Committee on the COVID-19 Pandemic — Sixth Meeting

**Body:** House of Commons Special Committee on the COVID-19 Pandemic  
**Meeting:** Sixth (videoconference)  
**Chair:** Hon. Anthony Rota (Nipissing–Timiskaming, Liberal)  
**Purpose:** This document summarises the sixth meeting for a policy reader. The session opened with a petition period, then proceeded to direct questioning of the Prime Minister and cabinet ministers. No specific calendar date is recorded in the transcript.

### Block B — Ministerial Questioning

#### 1. CERB fraud and government accountability

Dan Albas (CPC) and Alain Rayes (CPC) pressed on a reported deputy-minister memo instructing CRA staff to process CERB applications despite fraud flags, and on whether 200,000 applications had been flagged as potentially fraudulent. PM Trudeau repeatedly framed the issue as a deliberate speed-versus-precision trade-off — emphasising the priority of getting money to Canadians quickly and committing to "clean it up after the fact" — but did not directly answer whether he or his office authorised the memo. When Rayes asked Minister Lebouthillier the same question in a single yes-or-no form, she flatly answered: "The answer is no." The two on-the-record accounts are in direct tension.

#### 2. Large employer emergency financing facility and tax havens

Gabriel Ste-Marie (BQ) questioned whether the facility's conditions effectively excluded companies using legal tax havens, arguing that Bay Street banks and multinationals engaged in legal tax avoidance would still qualify. PM Trudeau stated the facility would assess applicants' tax structures case by case and that large banks were not expected to need the funds. Ste-Marie also raised capping Visa/Mastercard interchange fees at 0.3% (citing European and Australian caps) to relieve merchant costs; the PM's response did not commit to that measure.

#### 3. CERB gaps and housing/homelessness

Jenny Kwan (NDP) identified a specific eligibility gap: single parents relying on court-ordered spousal and child support income disrupted by COVID-19 were not eligible for CERB. PM Trudeau acknowledged that gaps exist and referenced the Canada Child Benefit increase but did not confirm a fix to the spousal-support gap. Kwan also called for matching federal funds to provincial housing initiatives for homeless people during the pandemic and highlighted that Vancouver East — home to Canada's third-largest urban indigenous population — received less than 2% of the pandemic urban indigenous housing funding.

#### 4. Service Canada operational issues

Candice Bergen (CPC) reported constituent cases of mail sent to Service Canada in mid-March remaining unopened as of the session, including a doctor's note required for a non-COVID benefit. Minister Hussen acknowledged the redeployment of thousands of staff and the closure of a number of Service Canada centres due to COVID-19 concerns, but said he was unaware of unopened mail being a widespread problem and offered to investigate the specific case.

#### 5. Firearms legislation — Iver Johnson rifle

Candice Bergen challenged the prohibition of the Iver Johnson single-shot rifle, arguing it is a standard farm and hunting firearm with no military design and disputing that front-line police supported the ban. Minister Blair defended the prohibition by citing the Canadian Association of Chiefs of Police — which called military assault rifles weapons "produced for the sole purpose of killing people in large numbers" — and Ontario police leaders' statements supporting bans on military-designed weapons. Bergen disputed that the Iver Johnson met that description.

#### 6. CERB repayment rules for employees returning to work

Alain Rayes pressed for a clear answer on whether an employee who returns to work mid-month must repay the full $2,000 monthly CERB payment or only $500 per week worked. The programme had been running for seven weeks at the time of questioning. Neither Minister Lebouthillier nor Minister Qualtrough gave a direct numerical answer; Qualtrough stated that Service Canada would work with individuals case by case to avoid undue hardship. On a related question, Qualtrough confirmed that CERB is designed for workers not working due to COVID-19 and that existing federal and provincial labour processes govern work refusals.

#### 7. Wet'suwet'en MOU and consultation

Jamie Schmale (CPC) raised the Wet'suwet'en elected chiefs' press release asking the government to delay a joint announcement on a memorandum of understanding on rights and title — scheduled for May 14 — until the community had adequate opportunity to review it. Minister Bennett said engagement was ongoing, that any solution must be Wet'suwet'en-led, and that elected chiefs had seen the agreement, but did not confirm a delay. Schmale also raised a procedural complaint that Conservative members had been shut down repeatedly at the COVID committee when raising the Wet'suwet'en issue; Bennett disputed that characterisation.

#### 8. Seniors' pandemic support

Stephanie Kusie (CPC) questioned why a one-time $500 OAS top-up payment was being announced only three months into the pandemic. Minister Schulte noted that a prior GST credit top-up — approximately $375 for single seniors and over $500 for couples — had already been delivered, and that the new OAS payment was a further measure on top of that.

#### 9. PPE procurement and pandemic preparedness

Kusie cited Public Services and Procurement Canada figures showing that less than 6% of N95 respirator orders, less than 8% of surgical mask orders, and 1.4% of face shield orders had been filled, despite 1.8 billion units of PPE having been procured. She also raised concerns about faulty PPE from China (citing 34 Chinese suppliers) and provinces using expired national stockpile equipment. Minister Anand described a multi-source procurement strategy across domestic, US, and international suppliers, and stated that the primary focus was ensuring safe, effective equipment reached front-line health care workers.

#### 10. Seafood harvester support

Chris d'Entremont (CPC) raised two issues. First, that fishing enterprises — typically family-staffed operations — were excluded from the Canada Emergency Wage Subsidy due to its related-party rule, leaving harvesters without direct support. Minister Jordan acknowledged that because of the unique structure of fishing enterprises they were ineligible for many existing programmes, confirmed that direct support measures were in development, and said she hoped to have more to say shortly. Second, d'Entremont noted that catch-limitation conditions had been added to 2021 gulf lobster fishing licences without harvesters' knowledge or advisory-member input. Minister Jordan confirmed the conditions were added without her authorisation, that they had been immediately removed from the licence conditions, and that they would not recur.

#### 11. Trikafta access for cystic fibrosis patients

D'Entremont noted that Vertex Pharmaceuticals had not yet applied to Health Canada for approval of Trikafta — a treatment effective in approximately 90% of cystic fibrosis cases — and cited the death of Chantelle Lindsay, a 23-year-old Nova Scotian, from CF complications. Minister Hajdu confirmed that Vertex had not applied, noted that the special access programme had provided access to the majority of applicants who applied through it, and encouraged continued engagement with the manufacturer.

## Outcomes and Notable Moments

**Formal outcomes:** No motions, votes, study orders, or formal requests for documents or witnesses appear in the transcript. One ministerial commitment was placed on the record mid-session: Minister Jordan confirmed that catch-limitation conditions added to 2021 gulf lobster licences had been applied without her authorisation, had been immediately removed, and would not recur.

**Notable exchanges for a policy reader:** The three exchanges most significant for policy purposes are covered in detail under threads 1, 6, and 11 of Block B above. In brief: the conflict between PM Trudeau's framing of CERB fraud as a deliberate trade-off and Minister Lebouthillier's flat denial of any such instruction is on the record without resolution; Minister Qualtrough was unable to state a basic CERB repayment rule seven weeks into the programme; and Minister Hajdu confirmed that the current pathway to Trikafta for CF patients is the special access programme, not a Health Canada approval, because Vertex has not applied.
