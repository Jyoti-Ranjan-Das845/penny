# Read what the draft actually says

Use this for substantial or sensitive drafts and after a material change of meaning. It extends the cross-model workflow, not the user-facing document. Short messages can use its checks within the existing final pass.

## Cold reading

In a fresh session, give the reader only the exact draft and a line identifying the intended reader and relationship. Do not include the writer's intent, sources, previous reviews, or a hint about expected defects. Ask for a short account of what the reader would believe, what they would do, and what commitments the text implies. Do not force an action or commitment when there is none. Commit this result before revealing the evidence.

## Check against the source

Then supply the original request, raw relevant sources, corrections, and that cold takeaway. The reader checks the draft's meaning, not the writer's claim that it passed. Record material claims with the quoted span, source pointer, consequence, and one status:

- **Supported:** the source or the author's supplied facts license this meaning and strength.
- **Contradicted:** the source says something different; identify both passages.
- **Absent:** the available source does not settle it. This is a gap to assess, not an automatic rejection.

Keep status separate from action. Return **ship** when there is nothing material to fix, **revise** for a concrete defect, or **ask** for a consequential choice the evidence cannot resolve. Missing external verification does not invalidate the user's own facts in an editing task. Never invent a follow-up promise to fill a gap.

Check that supported commitments retain who, what, and when where those details matter. Proposed work must not become agreed work. Check likely takeaway, meaningful omissions, unnecessary content, and voice. A clean draft should receive “nothing material,” not a manufactured objection. A finding needs a reader consequence; stylistic preference alone is not a defect.

For a rewrite, the deliverable must be corrected, usable text. Passing back the author's original with bracketed problems or internal notes is not a completed revision. The reader reports defects or a small phrase suggestion; the lead writes the final artifact.

## After a revision

Use a fresh session after a substantial change. Form its cold takeaway before the evidence, then check current sources before showing the prior findings. Only then compare the old and new findings: were the problems fixed, did the edit introduce another, and did material supported facts survive? A deliberately irrelevant omission is not a regression. Recheck within the existing round limit; do not multiply calls just to complete a checklist.

Keep test controls outside the skill. Test both a draft with a known defect and its clean twin: catch the defect with the right status without flagging legitimate content. Record actual model, effort, input, and output. A fresh context reduces anchoring; it does not prove model-family diversity or correctness.
